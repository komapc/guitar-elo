# Graded guitar exams: what each grade requires and how many hours the boards assume

Research date: 2026-11-18. Board documents were downloaded and converted with `pdftotext`. Where a PDF's music glyphs did not convert, the page was rendered to an image and read directly. "Inference" means arithmetic or interpretation by the researcher, not a statement in the source.

## Q1. RSL Awards / Rockschool (electric guitar; bass and acoustic share the same qualification sizes): requirements per grade, plus GLH/TQT

### Takeaway
RSL's graded music qualifications have the same size table as every other UK board: Debut 8 GLH / 40 TQT, rising to Grade 8 at 54 GLH / 320 TQT. These figures apply to each grade separately and are the same for every instrument. Rockschool electric-guitar technical work tops out at 100 bpm in eighth notes (about 3.3 notes/s) at Grades 6–8. The progression across grades comes from adding new material: scales and modes, chord types, and Quick Study Pieces from Grade 6.

### Cited Findings
**Hours (Rockschool Bass Syllabus Specification 2018, "Unit Overview – Graded Examinations in Popular Music Performance"; hosted on a third-party school site, newschool.ie):**
- The table lists QAN / GLH / credits / TQT for each grade. Debut (Entry 3) 501/0370/2: 8 / 4 / 40. G1 501/0391/X: 12 / 6 / 60. G2 501/0646/6: 18 / 9 / 90. G3 501/0647/8: 18 / 12 / 120. G4 501/0389/1: 24 / 15 / 150. G5 501/0643/0: 24 / 18 / 180. G6 501/0390/8: 36 / 22 / 220. G7 501/0645/4: 48 / 27 / 270. G8 501/0648/X: 54 / 32 / 320 — [RSL Bass Syllabus Spec 2018 (newschool.ie copy)](https://www.newschool.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/RSL_Bass_Syllabus_Guide_2018_DIGITAL_31May2019.pdf)
- The Ofqual register entry for 501/0648/X reads: "RSL Level 3 Certificate in Graded Examination in Music Performance - Grade 8", RSL Awards Ltd, Level 3, "Available to learners", GLH 54, TQT 320, 32 credits. It gives no description of what the hours mean. The register title says "Graded Examination in Music Performance", while the 2018 bass spec calls it "Popular Music Performance" — [Ofqual Find a regulated qualification, 5010648X](https://find-a-qualification.services.ofqual.gov.uk/qualifications/5010648X)
- A search snippet (page not opened) attributes the same Debut figures (8 GLH, 4 credits, 40 TQT) and Grades 1–3 figures to the 2019 RSL Acoustic syllabus spec — [Scribd copy of Acoustic 2019 Syllabus Specification](https://www.scribd.com/document/500697923/Acoustic-2019-Syllabus-Specification-13thNov2020) (secondary, not verified)

**Electric guitar technical work and supporting tests.** Source: the rslawards.com grade pages. These pages carry 2018 and 2024 repertoire tabs, but the technical-exercise text has no version label. Its structure matches the 2012–2018 Syllabus Guide, and the 2024 spec itself was not obtained.
- **Debut:** scales at 70 bpm (C major; A and E minor pentatonic). Open chords A, D, C, G, Am, Em, each strummed once. A riff to a backing track. Sight reading is a 4-bar rhythm on the low E string in half and quarter notes at 70 bpm, with 90 s of preparation — [RSL Electric Guitar Debut](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-debut/)
- **Grade 1:** scales at 70 bpm (C major, A natural minor, A/E minor pentatonic, G major pentatonic). Two-note power chords. Open A, D, E, C, G and Am, Dm, Em. Riff at 70 bpm. Sight reading is 4 bars in A minor at 70 bpm in half and quarter notes. The alternative, Improvisation & Interpretation, is at 70–80 bpm — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 1](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-one/)
- **Grade 2:** scales at 80 bpm (C and G major; A and E natural minor; minor and major pentatonics). Three-note power chords. Open minor 7th chords (Am7, Dm7, Em7) and major 7th chords (Amaj7, Cmaj7, Dmaj7). Riff at 80 bpm. Sight reading at 70 bpm — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 2](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-two/)
- **Grade 3:** scales two octaves in quarter notes at 90 bpm, in G, A and B (major, natural minor, minor and major pentatonic, blues). One-octave arpeggios in quarter notes at 90 bpm. Major and minor barre chords in a continuous sequence. Dominant 7ths A7, C7, D7, E7. Riff at 90 bpm. Sight reading 75–85 bpm; improvisation 80–90 bpm — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 3](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-three/)
- **Grade 4:** scales two octaves "in quarter notes" at 80 bpm, prepared in two fingerings (A–D). Maj7, m7 and dom7 arpeggios at 80 bpm. 7th chords on both the E and A strings. Riff at 100 bpm. Sight reading is 8 bars at 80–90 bpm with a 2-bar improvised ending (rock, funk or blues) — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 4](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-four/)
- **Grade 5:** scales two octaves in eighth notes at 80 bpm (major, natural minor, minor and major pentatonic, blues, harmonic minor). One-octave arpeggios in quarter notes at 80 bpm. Triads in three inversions on the top three strings, in C. Riff at 100 bpm. Sight reading at 90 bpm (8 bars plus a 2-bar improvised ending). Improvisation at 90–100 bpm. Ear tests at 90 bpm (melodic recall; harmonic recall on I–IV–V–vi). Marks: pieces 60%, technical 15%, sight reading or improvisation 10%, ear tests 10%, general musicianship 5%. Exam lasts about 26 min — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 5](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-five/)
- **Grade 6:** scales and modes two octaves in eighth notes at 100 bpm. All five minor-pentatonic positions. Dorian and Mixolydian in two positions, transposable chromatically (G–B on the E string, C–E on the A string). 7th arpeggios over two octaves at 100 bpm. m7♭5 and diminished chords. Stylistic study. Quick Study Piece (a lead sheet with 3 minutes of study) — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 6](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-six/)
- **Grade 7:** arpeggios at 100 bpm in eighth notes. Major, minor and dominant 9th arpeggios and chords in two positions. The scales and modes list Dorian and Mixolydian — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 7](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-seven/); this conflicts with the 2012–2018 guide, which lists Lydian, Phrygian and jazz melodic minor at Grade 7 — [Rockschool Guitar Syllabus Guide 2012–2018](https://cloud.rslawards.com/file/download/rockschoolguitar2012syllabusnew.pdf)
- **Grade 8:** scales and modes two octaves in eighth notes at 100 bpm (whole tone, diminished, altered). Arpeggios at 100 bpm (7♯5, 7♭5, m7♭5, 7♯9, 7♭9, diminished). Altered dominant chords. Quick Study Piece. Exam lasts about 33 min — [RSL Electric Guitar Grade 8](https://www.rslawards.com/tabbed-content/electric-guitar-grade-eight/)
- **2012–2018 guide summary tables:** technical-work tempo ♩ = 70 / 70 / 80 / 90 for Debut / G1 / G2 / G3, 80 for G4–5, and 100 for G6–8. Sight reading ♩ = 70 (Debut–G2), 80 (G3), 80–90 (G4), 90 (G5), with 4 bars to G3 and 8 bars at G4–5. Improvisation and interpretation ♩ = 70–80 (G1), 80–90 (G2–3), 90–100 (G4–5). The Quick Study Piece at G6–8 is 12 bars (6 sight reading plus 6 solo) at ♩ = 80–200, with one listen and 3 min of practice — [Rockschool Guitar Syllabus Guide 2012–2018](https://cloud.rslawards.com/file/download/rockschoolguitar2012syllabusnew.pdf)

### Inferences
- If the RSL tempos are read with their stated note values, the speed of technical work in notes per second goes: G3 at 90 bpm in quarter notes = 1.5 notes/s; G5 at 80 bpm in eighth notes = 2.7 notes/s; G6–8 at 100 bpm in eighth notes = 3.3 notes/s. The website's "quarter notes" at Grade 4 (80 bpm, 1.33 notes/s) would be slower than Grade 3, which is probably a website inconsistency; the Grade 5 page says eighth notes at the same tempo. Rockschool's technical tempos are modest. The grade-to-grade difference comes mostly from vocabulary (modes, extended chords, positions) and from the pieces.
- Of all the bpm figures here, the Rockschool riff and sight-reading tempos are the most direct basis for a "can you play X at Y bpm" quiz question.

### Gaps
- The RSL 2024 Electric Guitar syllabus specification was not obtained; the AMEB-hosted PDF redirected. Any 2024 tempo changes are therefore unverified.
- The RSL Acoustic and RSL Classical Guitar specifications were not read; only the search snippet on Acoustic is cited above.
- Example piece titles per Rockschool grade were not extracted (the grade pages load repertoire dynamically).

## Q2. Trinity College London: Rock & Pop guitar and Classical Guitar, requirements and GLH/TQT

### Takeaway
Both Trinity guitar syllabuses print the same hours table, which splits TQT into GLH plus Independent Learning Hours: Initial 8+32 = 40 up to Grade 8 54+266 = 320. Rock & Pop has no scales. It is assessed through songs (with per-grade parameters such as tempo range, techniques and solo length) and a session-skills test. Classical Guitar sets minimum metronome speeds for scales, from ♩=80 at Initial (a scale to the 5th) up to ♩=136 at Grade 8.

### Cited Findings
**Hours (identical in both syllabuses).** The definition reads: "Total qualification time is an estimate of the average time a candidate spends with a teacher (guided learning hours) added to the average time spent learning independently… should be used as guidance only." Per grade, GLH / ILH / TQT: Initial 8 / 32 / 40; G1 12 / 48 / 60; G2 18 / 72 / 90; G3 18 / 102 / 120; G4 24 / 126 / 150; G5 24 / 156 / 180; G6 36 / 184 / 220; G7 48 / 222 / 270; G8 54 / 266 / 320 — [Trinity Rock & Pop Guitar syllabus, graded exams from 2018](https://trinitycollege.com/resource?id=7899); [Trinity Classical Guitar syllabus, graded exams from 2020](https://trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=8862)
- UCAS points: G6 Pass/Merit/Distinction 8/10/12; G7 12/14/16; G8 18/24/30 — [Trinity R&P Guitar 2018](https://trinitycollege.com/resource?id=7899)
- Trinity says the Classical Guitar syllabus no longer has an end date; updates will be announced 12 months ahead (search summary of a Trinity page, not opened) — [Trinity Classical Guitar page](https://trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music/grade-exams/classical-guitar)

**Rock & Pop guitar (from 2018). Own-choice song parameters per grade; these are song guidelines, not technical-work tempos:**
- **Initial:** 45 s–2 min. 60–130 bpm. Mainly crotchets and quavers. Simple keys and chords, two-note power chords. "Time to change between chords". No improvisation.
- **G1:** 1–2.5 min, 60–160 bpm. More full chords and some melodic sections.
- **G2:** 1.5–3 min, 50–160 bpm, occasional semiquavers. Right-hand damping, palm mute, slides, occasional hammer-on/pull-off, optional distortion.
- **G3:** 1.5–3.5 min, "full range of tempos". Three-note power chords, half or partial barre, "occasional use of full barre chords". Fingerstyle, quarter-tone to tone bends, vibrato. Basic rhythmic improvisation on a chord sequence.
- **G4:** 2.5–4 min. "More regular use of full barre chords", faster position shifts. Clean/distortion changes, four-bar improvised solos.
- **G5:** any key, mandolin-style picking, improvised solos of about 8 bars.
- **G6:** 3–4.5 min, pinch harmonics, effects, solos of about 12 bars.
- **G7:** solos of about 16 bars.
- **G8:** 3.5–5 min, "any standard guitar technique", "multiple improvised solos, any length".
- Source for all of the above — [Trinity R&P Guitar 2018](https://trinitycollege.com/resource?id=7899)

**Session skills (20 marks; the candidate chooses Playback or Improvising):**
- **Improvising:** Initial is 4 bars in D major or A minor, I, IV and V only, simple rock/pop. G1 adds C major/E minor (ballad, heavy rock). G2 adds A major/D minor (country). G3 is 8 bars with 7th chords, swung quavers, blues. G4 adds maj7/m7 chords, two chords per bar, reggae and R'n'B. G5 is 12 bars (B major, F minor). G6 is 16 bars in any key with a solo break of up to 2 bars. G8 adds time-signature changes and a solo break of up to 4 bars.
- **Playback:** Initial is 8 bars in 2-bar phrases, first position, no chords. G3 adds the blues scale, two-note chords, 2nd position and hammer-ons/pull-offs. G4 adds three-note chords, slides and palm muting. G5 is 12–16 bars, up to 5th position, with bends and vibrato. G6 is 16–20 bars, up to 7th position, with harmonics. G7 uses the full fretboard range. G8 is 20–24 bars in any key.
- Source — [Trinity R&P Guitar 2018](https://trinitycollege.com/resource?id=7899)

**Classical Guitar (from 2020).** The technical-work pathway has a technical exercise plus a choice of scales & arpeggios, studies or (at G8) concerto extracts. Minimum tempi were confirmed against the rendered page image; the note values of the scales are given in Trinity's separate *Guitar Scales, Arpeggios & Studies from 2016* book, not in the syllabus.
- **Initial:** G major or A minor scale "to the 5th", scale ♩=80, arpeggio ♩=100. Technical exercise ♩=80.
- **G1:** one-octave C/F major and E natural minor (thumb), scales ♩=56, arpeggios to the 5th ♩=112.
- **G2:** one octave, scales ♩=62, arpeggios ♩=94, chromatic scale from G ♩=94. Exercise ♩=86.
- **G3:** two octaves (C/A major, B natural/E harmonic minor) plus C major in thirds. Scales ♩=70, arpeggios ♩.=38.
- **G4:** two octaves plus chromatic and a dominant 7th arpeggio. Scales ♩=82, arpeggios ♩.=44, dominant 7th ♩=66. Exercise ♩=130.
- **G5:** scales ♩=92, arpeggios ♩.=50, broken thirds ♩=82. Exercise ♩=126.
- **G6:** scales ♩=106, arpeggios ♩.=58. Exercise ♩=126.
- **G7:** scales ♩=122, arpeggios ♩.=66. Exercise ♩=102.
- **G8:** scales ♩=136, arpeggios ♩.=72, interval scales (3rds, 6ths, 10ths, octaves) ♩=108. Concerto-extract option: Vivaldi RV 93 1st movement bars 24–51; Castelnuovo-Tedesco Concerto No. 1 Op. 99 cadenza; Villa-Lobos Concerto 2nd movement cadenza.
- Source for all of the above — [Trinity Classical Guitar syllabus from 2020](https://trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=8862)
- Exam formats: digital candidates play three pieces plus technical work, or four pieces on the repertoire-only pathway. Own compositions are allowed, and duets up to Grade 5 — [Trinity Classical Guitar syllabus from 2020](https://trinitycollege.com/resource/?id=8862)

### Inferences
- The ILH/TQT ratio is the board's implicit assumption about home practice versus lessons. It rises from 80% at Initial–G2 (32/40, 48/60, 72/90) to 83–87% at G3–G8. In other words, the boards assume roughly 4–7 hours of independent practice for every hour of lesson.
- The Trinity R&P bpm ranges ("60–130", "50–160") are bounds on song tempo, not speed requirements. Speed-type anchors are better taken from improvisation length (4 → 8 → 12 → 16 bars) and the technique lists (power chords → barre chords → bends/vibrato → pinch harmonics).

### Gaps
- The note values (crotchets or quavers) of Trinity Classical scales are not in the syllabus; they are in the 2016 Scales book, which was not available. A tempo-to-notes-per-second conversion for Trinity is therefore not possible. The drop in scale tempo from ♩=80 at Initial to ♩=56 at G1 suggests the subdivision changes between those grades.
- Trinity Acoustic Guitar (2020–2023 syllabus) was not examined in detail beyond its one-page flyer.

## Q3. ABRSM (classical guitar) and LCM (London College of Music) guitar grades, requirements and GLH/TQT

### Takeaway
ABRSM and LCM (UWLQ) publish the same hour sizes as Trinity and RSL: Initial 8/40 (ABRSM), then 12/60 at Grade 1 up to 54/320 at Grade 8. ABRSM's 2019 guitar speed table, read from the rendered page, implies scales running from about 1.6 notes/s at Grade 1 to 4.8 notes/s at Grade 8. LCM's Rock Guitar syllabus names concrete songs per grade, from "Smoke on the Water" at Grade 1 to "Satch Boogie" at Grade 8.

### Cited Findings
**ABRSM hours.** These come from ABRSM's Piano Practical Grades 2025–2026 syllabus, "Qualification specification", in a copy hosted by a third-party shop. The table covers ABRSM's instrument-generic "Practical Music" qualifications.
- GLH / TQT / credits: Initial 8 / 40 / 4; G1 12 / 60 / 6; G2 18 / 90 / 9; G3 18 / 120 / 12; G4 24 / 150 / 15; G5 24 / 180 / 18; G6 36 / 220 / 22; G7 48 / 270 / 27; G8 54 / 320 / 32. GLH is defined as time "Under the guidance of a teacher". TQT is "Under the guidance of a teacher and time spent preparing independently". The figures are "estimates of the average amount of time … guidance only". Example QANs: 603/6408/7 (G3) to 603/6413/0, "ABRSM Level 3 Certificate in Practical Music (Grade 8)" — [ABRSM Piano syllabus 2025–2026 (caswells-strings.co.uk copy)](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)

**ABRSM Guitar syllabus from 2019** (third-party copy; ABRSM states that scales, sight-reading and aural were unchanged from the previous syllabus):
- **Exam:** three pieces, one each from lists A, B and C. Scales and arpeggios are played "from memory… legato… in even notes". Sight-reading and aural tests. Grade 8 requires Grade 5 Theory, Practical Musicianship or a solo Jazz instrument as a prerequisite.
- **Speed table ("general guide"):**
  - Thumb-only scales: G1 ♩=96, G2 ♩=112.
  - Scales over 1–2 octaves (pattern in quavers): ♩ = 48 / 56 / 66 / 80 / 96 for G1–G5.
  - Two-octave scales (quavers): ♩ = 112 / 126 / 144 for G6–G8.
  - Three-octave scales (quaver triplet groups): ♩. = 76 / 84 / 96 for G6–G8.
  - Chromatic scales: ♩ = 66 / 80 / 96 / 112 for G3–G6; three octaves ♩. = 84 / 96 for G7–G8.
  - Interval scales: ♩ = 60 → 116 for G3–G8.
  - Arpeggios (three quavers per beat): ♩. = 40 / 42 / 44 / 48 / 56 / 63 / 69 / 76 for G1–G8.
  - Broken chords: ♩. = 54 → 88 for G3–G8.

- **Sight-reading parameters:** G1 is 4–6 bars in C, G or F major or A or E minor, first position, single line. G3 is up to 8 bars, 2nd position, simple thumb-and-finger broken chords. G5 is about 8–12 bars, up to 5th position, simple two-note chords. G6 goes above 5th position, with three-note chords and partial barrés. G7 is about 12–16 bars, with 12th-fret harmonics and four-note chords. G8 is about 16–20 bars with full barrés.
- **Grade 1 example pieces:** Jasmine Flower (arr. Batchelar), Sur le pont d'Avignon, Carulli Écossaise Op. 121 No. 5, Sor March, "Somethin' Stupid" (arr. Wright).

### Inferences
- The ABRSM pattern glyphs, read from the rendered image, are single-beamed quavers: "♩ ♫ ♫♫" for 1–2-octave scales, four beamed quavers for 2-octave scales, and three beamed quavers per dotted crotchet for 3-octave scales and arpeggios. On that reading, scales go G1 96 notes/min (1.6/s), G3 132 (2.2/s), G5 192 (3.2/s), G6 224 (3.7/s), G7 252 (4.2/s), G8 288 (4.8/s). The 3-octave figure at G8 (♩.=96 × 3 = 288/min) agrees with the 2-octave one (♩=144 × 2 = 288/min), which supports the quaver reading. Arpeggios go from 120 notes/min (G1) to 228 (G8).
- Grade 8 scale speed in notes per second therefore differs by board: ABRSM classical about 4.8 notes/s against Rockschool electric about 3.3 notes/s. A quiz should not treat "Grade N" as one speed across boards.

### Gaps
- The LCM Electric Guitar grades (a separate syllabus from Rock Guitar) were not read.
- No ABRSM document was found on abrsm.org itself; the piano-syllabus hours table and the guitar syllabus are third-party-hosted copies.
- The ABRSM guitar figures for Initial/Prep Test are not in the guitar syllabus. The ABRSM Initial 8/40 figure comes from the piano qualification specification.

## Q4. Ofqual register entries: TQT/GLH numbers and definitions (does TQT include home practice?)

### Takeaway
Yes. Under Ofqual's definition, TQT is GLH (time under a teacher's "Immediate Guidance or Supervision") plus directed but unsupervised study, which covers home practice. The figures are notional and are set per grade, with no statement that one grade's hours build on the previous grade's. All four boards publish an identical size band (TQT = 10 × credit value in every row), so these are one regulatory convention, not four independent estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Ofqual's definition: "Total Qualification Time is the number of notional hours which represents an estimate of the total amount of time that could reasonably be expected to be required in order for a Learner to achieve and demonstrate the achievement of the level of attainment necessary for the award of a qualification." TQT comprises "(a) the number of hours… assigned… for Guided Learning, and (b) an estimate of the number of hours a Learner will reasonably be likely to spend in preparation, study or any other form of participation in education or training, including assessment, which takes place as directed by – but, unlike Guided Learning, not under the Immediate Guidance or Supervision of – a lecturer, supervisor, tutor…" — [Ofqual, Total Qualification Time Criteria (2015)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5b7544eded915d0ad553404b/total-qualification-time-criteria.pdf). GOV.UK marks this document as withdrawn and superseded by the Ofqual Handbook (General Condition E7) — [GOV.UK publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/total-qualification-time-criteria) (status per search summary)
- The register entry for RSL Grade 8 (501/0648/X) shows GLH 54 and TQT 320, credits 32, "Available to learners", with no size narrative — [Ofqual register 5010648X](https://find-a-qualification.services.ofqual.gov.uk/qualifications/5010648X)
- The boards' own wording: Trinity says "average time a candidate spends with a teacher… added to the average time spent learning independently" — [Trinity R&P Guitar 2018](https://trinitycollege.com/resource?id=7899). ABRSM says "time spent preparing independently" — [ABRSM Piano 2025–26](https://caswells-strings.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ABRSM-Piano-syllabus-2025-2026.pdf)

## Q6. Surveys of how much pupils actually practise per grade, versus the official figures

### Takeaway
The best quantitative data found is the Hallam et al. UK survey of 3,325 young classical instrumentalists, which has no separate guitar group. Mean weekly practice rises from about 2 h at Grade 1 to about 7 h at Grade 8, with very wide spread (SD roughly equal to the mean). ABRSM's Making Music 2014 report does not give practice time by grade. Sloboda et al. (1996) is the classic source on cumulative practice per grade, but its numbers could not be verified here.

### Cited Findings
- Hallam, Varvarigou, Creech and Papageorgi, "Are there differences in practice depending on the instrument played?" (author accepted manuscript, UCL Discovery): 3,325 young people from beginner to conservatoire-entry level, classical instruments.
  - Table 2, mean weekly practice in minutes, all instruments, N = 2,559: Preliminary 160 (N 370); G1 116 (262); G2 132 (182); G3 140 (217); G4 192 (225); G5 226 (442); G6 232 (276); G7 314 (255); G8 427 (330).
  - Strings subgroup: G1 121, G5 240, G8 451. Keyboard: G8 561. Brass: G8 315. Woodwind: G8 364.
  - SDs are comparable to the means (e.g., G8 total SD 354).
  - Guitar does not appear as a group in the paper.
  - Source — [Hallam et al., UCL Discovery AAM](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10094194/3/Hallam_POM%20Practice%20time%20and%20strategies%20by%20instrument%20group_IP_MV%20ARC.pdf)
- The same paper's literature review cites a conservatoire-undergraduate study with a mean of 17 h/week (2 h 40 min/day). That is a different, adult population, not the 3,325 children — [Hallam et al.](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10094194/3/Hallam_POM%20Practice%20time%20and%20strategies%20by%20instrument%20group_IP_MV%20ARC.pdf)
- Sloboda, Davidson, Howe and Moore (1996, *British Journal of Psychology* 87:287–309) compared five groups of young musicians. Summarised by Barry & Hallam: the "best" group practised more at all ages, giving "large cumulative differences, although there was wide individual variation. Some individuals in all groups managed to attain grade examination passes on very little practice. There were others who did four times as much practice as the average to attain a given grade" — [Barry & Hallam 2002 (UCL Discovery)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1507397/1/Barry2002Practicing151.pdf)
- The same review cites Ericsson et al. (1993): by age 18, "good violinists 5,310 hours and the teachers 3,420 hours" of accumulated practice — [Barry & Hallam 2002](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1507397/1/Barry2002Practicing151.pdf)
- ABRSM *Making Music* 2014 contains no practice-time-by-grade table. A full-text search for "practi" finds only teacher-reported barriers: "63% of teachers cite lack of practice and motivation as a barrier", and 67% use practice diaries — [Making Music 2014 (Youth Music copy)](https://www.youthmusic.org.uk/sites/default/files/2014_Making_Music_web_version_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Hallam weekly minutes against TQT, assuming about one year per grade and 40–52 practice weeks (both assumptions):**
  - G1-level pupils practise about 1.9 h/week, roughly 78–101 h/year, against TQT 60.
  - G5-level pupils: about 3.8 h/week, roughly 150–196 h/year, against TQT 180.
  - G8-level pupils: about 7.1 h/week, roughly 285–370 h/year, against TQT 320.
  - Taken together, the official TQT figures are roughly in line with what typical UK exam-takers report practising per year, if a grade takes about a year. The wide SDs mean any single learner can be off by a factor of 2–4, which is consistent with Sloboda's "four times the average".
- For a log-hours curve, the survey suggests weekly practice roughly doubles from Grade 1 to Grade 5 and doubles again by Grade 8.

### Gaps
- The widely repeated figure that Sloboda et al. (1996) found about 3,300 cumulative hours to reach Grade 8 could not be verified: the paper is paywalled, and the only hit was a garbled snippet. Do not use it without checking the original's tables.
- No guitar-specific practice-by-grade survey was found.
- No data was found on the typical time (years) per grade. Hallam's Table 2 gives practice per week at each level, not time spent at that level.
- The Hallam paper's journal and year were not confirmed; the filename suggests *Psychology of Music*.

## Q7. Concrete per-grade benchmarks usable as quiz anchors (tempo, chord changes, repertoire)

The firmest numeric anchors are the official scale speeds (ABRSM, Trinity Classical, LCM Classical, Rockschool) and the Rockschool sight-reading and riff tempos. The best qualitative anchors are the chord-vocabulary milestones (barre chords at Grade 3, 7th chords at G2–4, extended chords at G7–8), the improvisation lengths, and the named songs and pieces per grade.

### Inferences
- **Notes per second for scales (converted from the tempos above):** about 1.5–1.6/s around Grade 1–3, about 2.7–3.2/s around Grade 5, and 3.3–4.8/s at Grade 8 depending on board. - **Barre-chord fluency in songs** is consistently placed at Grade 3–4 by Trinity, RSL and LCM. By summed TQT (an assumption) that is roughly 270–460 notional hours from scratch.

### Gaps
- No board sets a strumming tempo or a "chord changes per minute" benchmark. Rhythm-guitar anchors must be inferred from song tempo ranges (Trinity) or riff tempos (RSL 70→100 bpm).
