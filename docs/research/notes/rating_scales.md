## 1. Piece-difficulty scales: Henle, RCM, ABRSM/Trinity/LCM, Ultimate Guitar, and where the classic guitar pieces sit

### Takeaway
Henle's 1–9 scale is the best-documented scale for a publisher's pieces, but it covers piano and orchestral instruments, not guitar. Each instrument's scale is set by one named expert. For classical guitar, the RCM Classical Guitar Syllabus (2018 edition) is the best public, official anchor. It places Lágrima at Level 4, Spanish Romance at Level 5, Adelita at Level 6, Recuerdos de la Alhambra at Level 9 and Asturias at Level 10, on a ladder of Preparatory, Levels 1–10 and then the ARCT diploma. Popular-music tab sites (Ultimate Guitar) use coarse labels that whoever submits the tab sets for that tab, not for the song.

### Cited Findings
**Henle Verlag (piano, violin; also flute, cello, clarinet browsing) [primary, read]**
- Henle uses nine levels in three bands: 1–3 easy, 4–6 medium, 7–9 difficult. — [Henle: Levels of Difficulty](https://www.henle.de/Levels-of-Difficulty/)
- Who sets them:
  - Piano levels were set up by Rolf Koenen (page © 2010). Jacob Leuschner (© 2025) has taken over.
  - Violin levels are by Ernst Schliephake (© 2013) and follow Koenen's nine-level piano model.
  - Flute has an introductory essay by András Adorján but no table on the page.
  - The page has no guitar scale.
  - — [Henle](https://www.henle.de/Levels-of-Difficulty/)
- Criteria:
  - Koenen weighs the number of fast or slow notes, chord sequences, compositional complexity, rhythmic complexity, how hard the piece is at first sight-reading, and how easily its structure can be understood.
  - Leuschner weighs formal, rhythmic and harmonic complexity, texture density, technique (fingering, pedalling, chords and leaps, tonal demands) and the "requirements of creative maturity."
  - Koenen: "My assessment is measured by the ability to prepare a piece for performance."
  - — [Henle](https://www.henle.de/Levels-of-Difficulty/)
- Anchors:
  - Level 1 is for pieces "ideally playable after a year of good piano instruction."
  - Level 9 is "for the relatively few works that combine exceeding structural complexity with extreme technical demands."
  - — [Henle](https://www.henle.de/Levels-of-Difficulty/)
- Example piano pieces by level. — [Henle](https://www.henle.de/Levels-of-Difficulty/)

### Inferences
- For a "hardest piece you can perform" question, RCM 2018 levels are the most defensible public anchor. In ascending order:
  - Lágrima (4)
  - Spanish Romance (5)
  - Adelita (6)
  - La catedral Preludio (8)
  - Recuerdos (9)
  - Asturias (10)
  - Lágrima ranking below Spanish Romance may surprise players; RCM's placement says so.
- The rough crosswalk is RCM 10 ≈ ABRSM 8 (pianolibrary), with ARCT/LTCL above that. Grade equivalences between boards are rough; Lágrima is "Grade 4" on one site, "ABRSM Grade 5" in Gramophone, and RCM Level 4.
- Henle cannot anchor guitar directly. Its useful contribution is the time anchor that Level 1 ≈ one year of good instruction.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm official ABRSM or Trinity guitar placements for Spanish Romance, Lágrima, Recuerdos or Asturias. ABRSM lists change each syllabus cycle, and no primary list was opened.
- A search snippet claimed "Henle Level 1 ≈ RCM/ABRSM Grade 3", but I could not tie it to a specific URL, so I left it out of the findings. If true, it would conflict with the other crosswalks above.
- **Not investigated:** Songsterr difficulty, method-book level systems (e.g., Hal Leonard, Noad, Delcamp), and the guitartricks or guitarmasterclass difficulty pages. Their help pages appeared in results but were not opened. I don't know whether Songsterr has a difficulty field at all.

## 2. Apps and games with skill levels: Yousician, Rocksmith, Guitar Hero/Rock Band, rhythm games (osu!, Etterna), Duolingo

### Takeaway
None of the music-learning apps I checked publish a rating methodology. Their "levels" are curriculum positions (Yousician) or opaque per-phrase adaptation (Rocksmith's Dynamic Difficulty/mastery). Rhythm games have the only fully documented player-rating formulas, and those are weighted sums of best scores on difficulty-rated charts (osu! pp, Etterna skillsets), not Elo.

### Cited Findings
- **Yousician:**
  - The guitar path has "10 complete playing levels" divided into themes. Piano has 9 levels in Classical and Pop plus 5 in Knowledge. — [Yousician help: guitar learning path](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/206930639-How-the-guitar-learning-path-works); [piano syllabus](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/204697992-How-the-piano-syllabus-works) [snippet; help pages returned 403]
  - In 2021 Yousician moved from separate levels per topic (e.g., rhythm, lead) to "one overall master level." — [Yousician blog, Feb 2021](https://yousician.com/blog/whats-new-in-yousician-february-2021-update) [snippet]
  - Placement is reportedly a self-report at sign-up (instrument + current skill), per third-party reviews. — [Guitar Chalk review](https://www.guitarchalk.com/yousician-review/) [snippet]
- **Rocksmith 2014:**
  - I found no official formula.
  - Community sources say mastery is predicted per phrase. 100% mastery equals RS2012 "Master mode" (notes hidden). Mastery and accuracy are separate stats. — [CustomsForge: mastery and accuracy](https://customsforge.com/topic/1058-mastery-and-accuracy); [The Riff Repeater](https://theriffrepeater.com/rocksmith-remastered-screenshots-player-stats/) [snippet]
- **Guitar Hero:**
  - Easy uses only the first three fret buttons, Medium four, and Hard all five. Expert adds notes but no new frets.
  - "Beginner" was added in Guitar Hero World Tour.
  - — [Guitar Hero wiki: Easy](https://guitarhero.fandom.com/wiki/Easy) [snippet]
- **Rock Band:**
  - Community career guides describe per-instrument tiers from "Warmup" (tier 1) to "Impossible" (tier 9). Tier counts and names vary by game and instrument. There is also a separate band tier.
  - Community-quoted Harmonix explanations say on-disc tiers were partly set to make a setlist flow, and DLC was rated relative to other DLC. — [Rhythm Gaming World](https://rhythmgamingworld.com/?p=389067); [ludo.guide Rock Band](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/rock-band) [snippet; not Harmonix documentation]
- **osu! performance points (pp) [primary, read]:**
  - Each score's pp comes from the beatmap's per-mode difficulty (aim, speed, accuracy, strain) and the player's performance.
  - Total pp = Σ p·0.95^(n−1) over the player's best scores, ranked. Example: top scores 110/100/100/90/80 weight to ≈110/95/90/77/65.
  - Bonus pp = 416.6667·(1 − 0.995^min(N,1000)) for N ranked maps with a score, up to 413.894. About 137 scores reach half the bonus.
  - — [osu! wiki: Performance points](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Performance_points)
- **Etterna:**
  - Charts and players are rated on seven skillsets: stream, jumpstream, handstream, stamina, jackspeed, chordjack, technical.
  - Player skillset ratings aggregate individual score ratings, with parameters 1.05/0.1 in the port. Overall aggregates the seven, with parameters 1.125/0.1.
  - Source is the Rust port of Etterna's calculator, not official docs. — [docs.rs etterna rating_calc.rs](https://docs.rs/etterna/0.1.0/src/etterna/rating_calc.rs.html) [snippet]
- **Duolingo Birdbrain (language courses):**
  - It estimates both exercise difficulty and learner proficiency and updates both after every exercise: a wrong answer lowers ability and raises difficulty. That is Elo/IRT-like.
  - Nothing found says the Duolingo Music course (iOS, Oct 2023) uses it.
  - — [Duolingo blog: Introducing Birdbrain](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain); [Engadget on Duolingo Music](https://www.engadget.com/duolingos-gamified-music-lessons-are-launching-this-fall-050009231.html) [snippet; Duolingo claims are marketing/engineering-blog]

### Inferences
- osu!'s formula is a ready-made template for a repertoire question, used by analogy (not Elo):
  - The strength term is dominated by your hardest pieces with geometric decay (0.95^n).
  - Repertoire size adds a saturating bonus (1 − 0.995^N).
  - This matches "hardest piece" plus "repertoire size" as two quiz inputs.
- Rocksmith and Yousician give no external, citable scale, so they can't anchor quiz answers numerically. They can only serve as examples ("Yousician level X") without a known mapping.

### Gaps
- Not investigated: Simply Guitar, Fender Play and Songsterr skill systems.
- Not found: Yousician's placement or scoring method and Rocksmith's official mastery formula.
- I don't know whether any guitar app publishes an Elo-like learner rating. I found none.

## 3. Research on automatic or data-driven piece-difficulty estimation, and agreement between experts

### Takeaway
Piece-difficulty estimation is an active topic in music information retrieval (MIR), mostly on piano and built on Henle's single-expert labels. It is hard: the best 9-class accuracy on Henle levels is about 40%, though predictions are usually within one level. For guitar, an ISMIR 2023 rubric (7 criteria, 0–3 each) scored with an IRT partial-credit model shows that song "playability" is a reliable single dimension. I found no published inter-expert agreement figures for piece-difficulty ratings.

### Cited Findings
**Piano (Ramoneda et al., UPF/Sogang) [primary, read unless marked]**
- **CIPI ("Can I Play It?") dataset:**
  - Labels were scraped for 2,830 piano works from the Henle website. Henle's 1–9 rankings "are annotated by Prof. Rolf Koenen."
  - 652 pieces were matched to public-domain MusicXML, covering 29 composers and 9 levels.
  - The abstract says the labels were "reviewed and corrected by an expert pianist." The method section shows what that review was:
    - It was "manual best candidate selection or title rejection" of score–label matches by "a classical pianist with more than 20 years of playing", over "more than 100 hours of work".
    - It discarded 58% of MuseScore, 42% of Craig Sapp and 90% of Mutopia automatic matches, leaving 394 + 228 + 30 = 652 scores.
    - The interface functions listed (pick the best match, flag a mismatch, rate score quality 1–4) include no re-grading of difficulty. The difficulty labels therefore remain Henle's single-expert grades.
  - The authors also say crowd labels like 8notes' lack "standard criteria."
  - — [Ramoneda et al., "Combining piano performance dimensions for score difficulty classification", arXiv 2306.08480 / Expert Systems with Applications 2024, doi 10.1016/j.eswa.2023.121776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08480)
- **CIPI best results (ensemble):** 9-class balanced accuracy (Acc-9) 39.5%, 3-class (Acc-3) 71.3%, within ±1 level (Acc±1) 87.3%, MSE 1.1. On Mikrokosmos-difficulty (MKD, 147 pieces), Acc-3 76.4%. — [arXiv 2306.08480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08480)
- **Mikrokosmos-difficulty (MKD):** 3 levels from a six-book practice series (books 1–2 beginner, 3–4 intermediate, 5–6 professional; per the snippet the labels come from Bartók and the publishers). It is most likely the dataset of Ramoneda et al.'s ICASSP 2022 fingering-based difficulty paper, which found fingering features predictive. — [Vélez Vásquez et al. related-work summary](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf); [ICASSP 2022 paper page](https://deepai.org/publication/score-difficulty-analysis-for-piano-performance-education-based-on-fingering) [snippet]
- **"Towards Explainable and Interpretable Musical Difficulty Estimation" (RubricNet, arXiv 2408.00473, 2024):**
  - CIPI Acc-9 41.4%, MSE 1.7. MKD Acc-3 79.6%.
  - Manual labelling "is laborious and might lead to subjective errors."
  - A Chopin Nocturne op. 9/3 is labelled 7 but predicted 2. The authors attribute this to polyrhythms and dynamics the descriptors miss.
  - 23% of scores lack tempo.
  - — [arXiv 2408.00473](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00473v1)
- **PSyllabus:**
  - 7,901 piano pieces, 11 difficulty levels, 1,233 composers. Called "the first audio-based difficulty estimation dataset".
  - IEEE/ACM TASLP DOI 10.1109/TASLPRO.2025.3539018.
  - The abstract page does not say where the 11 labels come from.
  - — [Ramoneda et al., arXiv 2403.03947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03947)

**Guitar [primary, read unless marked]**
- **Vélez Vásquez, Baelemans, Driedger (Chordify), Zuidema, Burgoyne, "Quantifying the ease of playing song chords on the guitar," ISMIR 2023 — the rubric:**
  - Seven criteria, each scored 0 (very easy) to 3 (very difficult), with weights:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Uncommonness of chord | 3 |
| Chord finger positioning | 3 |
| Chord fingering difficulty | 2 |
| Repetitiveness | 2 |
| Right-hand complexity | 2 |
| Chord progression time | 1 |
| Beat difficulty (syncopes/ghost notes) | 0 |

  - Built from interviews with guitar teachers and experts.
  - — [ISMIR 2023 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)
- **Same paper — data and IRT model:**
  - Data: 200 songs from the McGill Billboard set (Hot 100, 1958–1991), annotated by **one** guitar expert.
  - They fit partial-credit IRT models (Stan).
  - Reliability: 0.74 (simple partial credit), 0.84 (generalised), 0.86 (extended). They recommend the generalised model; values ≥0.7 are "desirable."
  - The simple 3–3–2–2–2–1–0 weights fall within the 90% credible intervals.
  - This reliability is **one annotator's internal consistency across criteria**, not inter-expert agreement.
  - — [ISMIR 2023 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)
- **Song2Guitar (ISMIR 2017)** generates solo-guitar covers with difficulty in mind. Its factors include average index-finger movement and average fret spread, tested with proficient guitarists. — [ISMIR 2017 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000041.pdf) [snippet]
- **MIDI-to-Tab (ISMIR 2024):** 15 experienced guitarists rated the playability of 4-bar jazz excerpts. The model beat commercial tools but stayed below ground truth. — [ISMIR 2024 poster](https://ismir2024program.ismir.net/poster_175.html) [snippet]
- **Earlier work:** a 2012 method using MusicXML and seven instrument-agnostic criteria (ref [14] in the ISMIR 2023 paper; likely Sébastien et al.'s "Score Analyzer," ISMIR 2012). — [ISMIR 2023 paper, §2](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2023/paper/000086.pdf)

**Agreement among raters (adjacent evidence; about performances, not pieces)**
- Passarotto, Altenmüller & Müllensiefen (2023): aggregate expert ratings of music *performances* correlated r = .786 with ratings from non-experts. Musicians' self-assessments were largely unrelated to peer ratings. — [Goldsmiths Research Online](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/33665) [snippet]
- pianolibrary.org: "there is no such thing as an objective or fundamentally correct difficulty scale." — [pianolibrary.org](https://www.pianolibrary.org/about/difficulty-scale/) [snippet]

### Inferences
- Henle labels are one expert's judgement per instrument, and CIPI inherits that. The "ground truth" in the piano difficulty literature is therefore single-rater, and model accuracy of about 40% at 9 classes (about 87% within ±1) partly reflects label noise.
- A quiz asking "hardest piece" can't expect better than about ±1 level precision, and the papers suggest even experts shouldn't be held to more.
- The playability rubric has 7 criteria, scored 0–3 and weighted 3-3-2-2-2-1-0. It could be repurposed as self-assessment items for rhythm-guitar skill.

### Gaps
- Searched for and not found: inter-expert agreement statistics for *piece* difficulty (e.g., two publishers or two teachers rating the same pieces). Also not found: the PSyllabus label source. CIPI's expert review was match-and-quality filtering, not relabelling, so CIPI offers no agreement data either.
- No guitar dataset comparable to CIPI with grade labels (e.g., RCM/ABRSM levels paired with scores) turned up.

## 4. Elo, Glicko and IRT applied to music (players, pieces, assessment)

### Takeaway
In two Elo-specific queries and two comparative-judgement queries, I found no published use of Elo or Glicko to rate musicians or pieces, and no music app that advertises Elo. IRT and Rasch models, by contrast, are established in music:
- adaptive listening-ability tests (Harrison, Collins & Müllensiefen 2017);
- many-facet Rasch models of performance juries (Wesolowski; Engelhard and colleagues);
- the 2023 guitar-playability partial-credit model.

Pelánek (2016) shows that Elo with a learner-vs-item "match" is equivalent in form to the Rasch model and gives nearly the same estimates. It is a practical engine if the quiz ever wants to calibrate items from user data.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelánek 2016:** "Applications of the Elo rating system in adaptive educational systems," *Computers & Education* 98:169–179, doi 10.1016/j.compedu.2016.03.017. — [accepted manuscript PDF](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf) [primary, read]
  - Model: P(correct) = 1/(1+e^−(θs−di)). Updates: θs := θs + K·(correct − P) and di := di + K·(P − correct). Initial values are 0.
  - "In this formulation the Elo rating system is closely related to the Rasch model (one parameter model) used in IRT… they differ only in the parameter estimation procedure."
  - Elo gives very similar estimates to joint maximum likelihood estimation (JMLE), which is "much more computationally demanding" and unsuited to online updates.
  - Previous work used K = 0.4.
  - Uncertainty functions: U(n) = a/(1+bn) with a = 1, b = 0.05 (Papoušek et al. 2014; Nižnan et al. 2015). Also U(n) = w0/(1+a·e^(bn)) with w0 = 0.2, b = 50, a 0.01–0.15 (Wauters et al. 2011).
  - Extensions: Glicko and TrueSkill. A normal-CDF variant corresponds to the Thurstone–Mosteller paired-comparison model.
  - The case study is geography facts. The word "music" does not appear in the manuscript (my grep), so the paper has no music application.
- **Harrison, Collins & Müllensiefen 2017,** *Scientific Reports* 7:3618: IRT, computerised adaptive testing and automatic item generation for a melodic discrimination test. Three-alternative forced choice; two calibration/validation rounds; "strong reliability and construct validity." — [Goldsmiths](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/20570); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/3e936ebbd19341c8b02a32c5fb303c6f) [snippet]
- **Many-facet Rasch measurement of music performance assessment:**
  - Wesolowski et al.: 24 expert raters showed differential rater functioning across middle-school, high-school, collegiate and professional performances. ### Inferences
- The quiz's question format (player answers "can play X" against items of graded difficulty) is exactly Pelánek's student-vs-item Elo/Rasch setup. With user data, the quiz could learn item difficulties (pieces, skills) and a player θ online, using U(n) = 1/(1+0.05n) as a starting K schedule.
- θ is on a logit scale. Mapping it to chess Elo is a linear rescale (chess uses base-10 with 400 points per factor of 10 in odds). That scale choice is cosmetic and would not need the hours detour.
- The music evidence suggests rater and item calibration matters more than the choice of Elo vs Glicko. Rasch studies repeatedly find rater severity effects, and Henle-style single-expert labels are the norm.

  - any Elo or Glicko rating of musicians or pieces, or Elo-based ear-training or sight-reading apps (2 queries: 1 standard, 1 extended);
  - ACJ or comparative judgement in music (2 standard queries).
  - This is a search-limited negative, not proof of absence. Database searches (ERIC, RILM, Google Scholar) were not done.
- I did not open the Gold-MSI item wording or norms for its practice-hours items. That would be the most directly reusable published item set for "hours/years practiced."

## 5. Does any source relate a piece's grade to the practice hours needed?

### Takeaway
No source ties a specific *piece* to hours. Two sources tie *grades* to hours:
- UK regulated-qualification "Total Qualification Time" (TQT), a notional per-grade time: LCM gives 60 h for Grade 1 rising to 320 h for Grade 8.
- Sloboda et al. 1996, whose actual-practice figure of about 3,300 h to reach Grade 8 is reported only in secondary sources that conflict on whether it was the top group's average or held for every group.

One verified point is that the hours needed per grade vary hugely: some students needed about 4× the average (Barry & Hallam 2002, summarising Sloboda et al.). Henle supplies one time anchor: Level 1 ≈ one year of good instruction.

### Inferences
- **Summing LCM TQT for Grades 1–8 gives about 1,410 h** of notional study (60+90+120+150+180+220+270+320), of which 234 h is GLH, i.e., lessons.
  - TQT is per-grade and assumes the previous grade is held, so the cumulative sum is my construction, not an official figure.
  - It is about 0.43× the ~3,300 h actual-practice figure attributed to Sloboda et al. That gap is consistent with TQT being a regulatory minimum or average rather than measured practice.
  - Coincidentally, ~1,410 h is close to the quiz's existing calibration point (1,404 h).
- **Chaining guitar pieces to hours is not reliable with what was found:**
  - The crosswalks are piano-derived: RCM 10 ≈ ABRSM 8 (pianolibrary) and RCM ≈ 1.25 × ABRSM grade (forum heuristic).
  - Two of my own findings contradict them:
    - Lágrima is RCM 4, which by ×1.25 would be about ABRSM 3, yet Gramophone calls it ABRSM Grade 5.
    - Recuerdos is RCM 9, which would be about ABRSM 7, yet Trinity lists it at LTCL diploma level, above Grade 8.
  - Any per-piece hour figure would therefore inherit an error of about ±2 grades. On the LCM cumulative-TQT curve, that is roughly ±300–600 h in the middle grades.
  - The quiz could use the RCM level only as an ordinal anchor, and calibrate hours separately.
- **The two Grade-8 hour figures are not comparable bounds.**
  - About 1,410 h is cumulative notional TQT, which is my sum of regulatory estimates.
  - About 3,300 h is actual practice from an unverified secondary attribution to Sloboda.
  - Their gap (~2.3×) shows how far apart the two kinds of source are, not a confidence interval.

### Gaps
- I could not read the original Sloboda et al. (1996) grade-by-hours table. The 3,300 h figure, and whether it held across groups, needs the paper itself (doi 10.1111/j.2044-8295.1996.tb02591.x).
- I found no ABRSM or Trinity primary TQT table, only LCM's. I also found no guitar-specific hours data and no RCM hours guidance; the syllabus has none.
- No source maps individual guitar pieces to hours directly.
